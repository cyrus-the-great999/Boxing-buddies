3. Risks of sparring and combat sports — please read

Boxing and sparring are dangerous. Combat sports carry an inherent and significant risk of injury, including but not limited to cuts, bruises, broken bones, dental and eye injuries, concussions and other traumatic brain injuries, long-term neurological damage, heart or other medical events, permanent disability, and death. These risks exist even when participants are experienced, use proper equipment, and follow the rules, and they can be increased by mismatches in weight, skill, or intensity, by fatigue, by pre-existing medical conditions, and by the conduct of other people.

To reduce risk, we strongly recommend that you:

  • Spar only under supervision at an established boxing gym, with a qualified coach or trainer present;
  • Get medical clearance from a physician before sparring, and stop and seek medical attention if you are injured or experience symptoms such as headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, or vision changes;
  • Wear proper protective gear, including appropriately sized and weighted gloves, headgear, a mouthguard, hand wraps, and a groin protector (and chest protection where appropriate);
  • Agree on intensity, rules, and limits with your partner before you begin, and stop immediately if either person wants to stop;
  • Spar with partners of a similar weight and experience level, and be honest about your own experience;
  • Not spar if you are ill, injured, impaired by alcohol or drugs, or have recently had a concussion; and
  • Follow the gym's rules and any applicable laws or regulations.

These recommendations are general and are not a substitute for professional coaching or medical advice. Following them does not make sparring safe.
